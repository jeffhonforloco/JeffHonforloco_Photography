A beauty photography session is unlike any other type of photoshoot. The camera gets close — really close — and every detail of skin, makeup, and lighting is magnified. Proper preparation makes the difference between images you love and a session that feels stressful.

As a Providence beauty photographer, I've guided hundreds of clients through this process. Here's exactly how to prepare so you walk in confident and walk out with stunning images.

Two Weeks Before: Skin Preparation

Great beauty photography starts with great skin. Two weeks before your session, focus on hydration — drink plenty of water and use a good moisturizer. Avoid trying new skincare products; this is not the time to experiment with something that might cause a reaction.

If you get facials, schedule your last one at least 5-7 days before the shoot. Fresh facials can leave redness or cause breakouts. You want your skin calm and settled on shoot day.

One Week Before: The Details

Confirm your wardrobe and concepts with your photographer. For beauty work, clothing is minimal — the focus is on your face — but necklines matter. Bring options: a black top, a nude/skin-tone top, and anything specific to your concept.

Get your hair trimmed if needed (not the day before — give it a few days to settle). If you color your hair, do it at least a week out so the color looks natural, not fresh-from-the-box.

The Night Before: Rest and Hydration

Sleep is the best beauty prep there is. Aim for 7-8 hours. Avoid alcohol — it dehydrates skin and causes puffiness. Drink water, eat clean, and get to bed early.

Prep your shoot bag: wardrobe options, any specific jewelry or accessories discussed, your usual makeup for touch-ups (even if a makeup artist is provided), and reference images if you have a specific look in mind.

Shoot Day: What to Expect

Arrive with clean, moisturized skin and no makeup (unless directed otherwise). If a makeup artist is booked, they'll take 45-60 minutes. Use this time to relax, review the shot list with your photographer, and get comfortable.

During the shoot, trust the direction. Beauty photography involves precise posing — chin angles, eye direction, lip position. Small adjustments make huge differences at this magnification. Your photographer is watching details you can't see.

Makeup: Less Is Often More

For natural beauty looks, makeup should enhance, not mask. The camera and retouching handle the rest. For dramatic editorial beauty, go bolder — strong eyes, bold lips, creative color. Discuss the look with your photographer beforehand so the makeup artist knows the direction.

One critical tip: avoid SPF in your makeup for studio shoots. It can cause flashback (white cast) under strobe lighting. Use non-SPF foundation and set with a non-reflective powder.

After the Session

Your photographer will typically deliver a proof gallery within a few days, with final retouched images following in 1-2 weeks. Beauty retouching is detailed work — skin texture preservation, color grading, and subtle shaping take time to do well.

When selecting your favorites, look at expression and connection first. Technical perfection matters less than an image that makes you feel something.
